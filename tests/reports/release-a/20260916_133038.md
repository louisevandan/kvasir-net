## Executed Steps

1. Added the `execution_groups.I0` contract, which runs I0-S/M/L in one LOAD in the order `case-00`, `case-04`, `case-06`.
   Per-request deadlines are 600,000/1,200,000/1,800,000ms, and the overall timeout is 3,900,000ms:
   their sum plus a 300,000ms reclaim margin.
2. `prepare-integrity-i0.py` selects only the three cases from the output of the existing H1 corpus/oracle materializer and creates a seal with
   `max_in_flight=1` and 1 LOAD and 1 UNLOAD.
3. Added `eligible_ms`, `send_started_ms`, `send_completed_ms` and `release_ms` to the event-drive request output.
   The I0 judge rejects any run in which the next request's eligibility precedes the previous RELEASE.
4. `judge-integrity-i0.py` binds the raw artifact, seal, GPU samples, resource snapshots, distributed execution evidence and cleanup
   through the artifact/config hashes. The head scheduler phase and the span statistics of the three stages are computed separately.
5. Ran the formatter and the full `p4-event-drive` tests on Spark with a limited build.

## Failures

The first remote formatter check detected a rustfmt line-break difference in one assertion line. Without changing product semantics or test assertions,
only the exact line break rustfmt proposed was applied. On the second run, the formatter and all Rust tests
passed.

In the first self-test of the I0 raw judge, a mutation that removed one GPU sample was accepted at the bundle creation stage.
The final judge could have rejected it, but intermediate outputs must also fail closed, so a 95% coverage lower bound
was added to the raw judge. After the fix, all 8 kinds, including that mutation, were rejected. This lesson is pinned in L072.
